Ntokozo Mbambo

South Africa's foremost gospel voice — a minister, songwriter, and trailblazer whose music has carried faith across continents for over two decades.

Biography

A Voice Shaped by Faith and Purpose

Ntokozo Mbambo was born on 6 November 1985 in Umlazi — a township in Durban, KwaZulu-Natal, that has produced a remarkable number of South Africa's finest musicians and public figures. She grew up in a deeply musical household; both her parents were worship leaders, and that environment of devotion and song shaped her sense of calling long before she ever set foot on a professional stage. Music was not a career choice for Ntokozo so much as an extension of who she already was.

Her professional journey began at just fifteen years old when she became the youngest-ever lead singer of the celebrated South African gospel ensemble Joyous Celebration in 2002. It was a baptism by fire — performing before thousands, recording albums, and learning the craft of gospel ministry in one of the country's most beloved groups. Over the thirteen years she spent with the ensemble, she rose from lead vocalist to voice coach, shaping the sound of the group and pouring her expertise into younger singers coming up behind her.

In 2011 she made the bold decision to step away and pursue a solo career. Signing with Koko Records, she released her live album Filled in 2012, recorded before a sold-out crowd at the Lyric Theatre in Johannesburg. The album went Double Platinum in South Africa — a statement-making debut that proved her audience would follow her wherever she led. Her 2015 album Spirit and Life yielded the signature hit "Jehovah Is Your Name," an ascending worship song that gathered over 31 million video views and cemented her status as a defining voice in African gospel.

Beyond recordings and concerts, Ntokozo has been a consistent voice for developing the next generation. She served as a judge on the SABC 2 gospel talent competition I Want to Sing Gospel and later appeared on Idols SA Season 17. In 2023 her album Lavish Worship swept three categories at the 29th South African Music Awards, and she received a Lifetime Honorary Award in Ghana recognising her continental impact. In July 2024 she sang the South African national anthem for the Springboks at Kings Park Stadium in Durban — a moment watched by the nation. Her music continues to move between the intimate and the monumental, always anchored in the same quiet conviction she discovered as a child in a worship-filled home in Umlazi.

Key Milestones

Here's a timeline of Ntokozo Mbambo's life and career:

  • 2001

    Debut Album — Bambelela

    Released her first studio album shortly after joining Joyous Celebration, combining traditional gospel themes with a contemporary sound.

  • 2002

    Youngest Lead Singer, Joyous Celebration

    Joined the acclaimed gospel choir at fifteen as their youngest-ever lead singer, beginning a 13-year tenure as performer and voice coach.

  • 2007

    Keep On Believing — Live Album

    A landmark live recording that earned nominations at the Metro FM Music Awards and the Crown Gospel Music Awards.

  • 2012

    Filled Goes Double Platinum

    Her debut solo live album, recorded at the Lyric Theatre in Johannesburg, achieved Double Platinum status after leaving Joyous Celebration.

  • 2015

    Spirit and Life — Breakout Album

    Produced the landmark hit "Jehovah Is Your Name," gathering over 31 million video views and winning multiple gospel music awards.

  • 2018

    20 Years in Music — Moments in Time

    Celebrated two decades of ministry with an intimate concert and a surprise album released on her birthday.

  • 2023

    Lavish Worship Sweeps the SAMAs

    Won Female Artist of the Year, Album of the Year, and Best Contemporary Faith Album at the 29th South African Music Awards.

  • 2023

    Lifetime Honorary Award — Ghana

    Received a Lifetime Honorary Award at the Praise Achievement Awards in Ghana, recognising her enduring continental impact.

  • 2024

    National Anthem for the Springboks

    Sang "Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika" before the Springboks at Kings Park Stadium, Durban — a moment of national honour.
